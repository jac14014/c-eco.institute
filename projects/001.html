INST.8.2 — Scientific Independence

Scientific independence is a foundational principle of the network. Scientific conclusions are determined by evidence and scientific process, not by external pressure or preference. Scientific independence shall be protected through: (a) scientific governance and review processes; (b) separation of scientific leadership from operational and project delivery pressure; (c) independent review where appropriate; (d) documentation and traceability of scientific findings and their basis; and (e) institutional commitment that scientific integrity takes precedence over convenience, favor, or institutional or external preference.

Scientific independence does not mean isolation, secrecy, or absence of external collaboration. It means that inconvenient scientific findings are not suppressed, and favorable findings are not promoted for reasons other than their scientific merit.

INST.8.3 — Calibration Framework

Calibration is the scientific process of ensuring that measurements, observations, data, models, methods, indicators, and findings are accurate, consistent, comparable, and fit for their intended scientific and operational purpose. Calibration applies to field observations and sensor data; monitoring systems and data; datasets and data sources; models and analytical methods; indicators and threshold-related metrics; and cross-domain and cross-Lab comparability. Calibration requirements shall be defined for the relevant evidence types and shall be maintained and documented through the applicable scientific governance and quality processes.

INST.8.4 — Technical Independence

Technical independence ensures that scientific and methodological determinations are not improperly influenced by technology constraints, software design, or platform administration. SIL 19×19 is software and does not make scientific judgments. The GIH administers SIL operationally but does not own it and does not have scientific validation authority. The Institute provides technology governance but does not allow technology governance to override scientific determination. The distinction between software-supported analysis and scientific judgment is fundamental and shall be preserved throughout the network.

INST.22.3 — Compliance vs. Scientific Determination

The distinction between compliance and scientific determination is fundamental. Compliance obligations shall be identified, assigned, satisfied, and reviewed; scientific determination shall be made by the appropriately constituted scientific functions, through the applicable scientific governance, review, validation, calibration, and independence processes. Compliance review shall not determine scientific findings; scientific determination shall not dispense with compliance obligations.
