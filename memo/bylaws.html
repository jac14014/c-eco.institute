Initial Bylaws
Missouri nonprofit corporation • Sole Incorporator adoption • September 4, 2026

Article I — Name, Purpose, and Governing Principles

Section 1.1 — Name

The name of the corporation is C-ECO Institute (the "Institute"), a nonprofit corporation organized under the laws of the State of Missouri.

Section 1.2 — Exempt Purposes

The Institute is organized exclusively for charitable, scientific, and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, including research, education, capacity building, public-interest standard development, Living Labs, and development and dissemination of tools for the anticipatory governance of irreversible and complex systemic risks.

Section 1.3 — Core Institutional Mission

The Institute shall advance the public-interest development, testing, stewardship, teaching, and responsible implementation of the c-ECO framework, including its public taxonomy, TDR–TFP methodology, pre-threshold governance protocols, Model Law research, SIL 19×19 software or successor systems, Living Labs, Fellowship, licenses, certifications, and seals. Nothing in this Section transfers ownership of Founder Background IP.

Section 1.4 — Integrated Governance Framework

The Institute shall establish and maintain an integrated governance framework to support its multi-stakeholder and scientific operations. Ultimate legal, financial, and fiduciary authority over all activities, components, and assets of the Institute shall reside exclusively in the Board of Directors, in accordance with Missouri law. Within this unified corporate structure, the Institute shall maintain the following functional components:(a) The C-ECO Institute (Corporate & Administrative Core): Responsible for general corporate administration, strategic personnel management, the c-ECO Fellowship program, operational administration of Living Labs, finance, general charitable operations, and the protective stewardship, administration, and licensing of the Institute’s intellectual property.(b) The c-ECO System (Technical Standards Committee): A standing committee established by the Board of Directors pursuant to these Bylaws, possessing operationally independent authority over scientific standards, technical calibration, infrastructure metrics, and framework integrity. The Board shall defer to the specialized technical and scientific determinations of the c-ECO System to ensure objective, data-driven calibration, provided such actions remain consistent with the Institute’s tax-exempt purposes and the Board's nondelegable fiduciary duties.(c) The C-ECO Observatory (Public Oversight Committee): A standing committee established by the Board of Directors responsible for independent transparency metrics, institutional public reporting, data-quality auditing, and facilitating broad civic and community participation in the Institute’s initiatives.(d) Global and Intergovernmental Cooperation: The Institute may actively cooperate with, advise, or enter into programmatic alignments with international organizations, sovereign states, and intergovernmental entities to advance global standards in ecological and contractual governance. The Institute may prepare its assets, open-source frameworks, and structural operational models for transition to or adoption by a future international treaty-based organization, subject to approved asset-transfer protocols compliant with Code Section 501(c)(3) and Missouri law.The c-ECO System and the C-ECO Observatory are functional subdivisions and standing committees of the Institute and do not possess separate legal personality under Missouri law unless explicitly organized as separate entities by lawful action of the Board of Directors.

Section 1.5 — Scientific Integrity and Operational Separation

To preserve institutional credibility and mitigate structural conflicts of interest, the Institute shall maintain internal governance arrangements that separate scientific calibration, technical certification, and data-quality evaluation from commercial, general operational, fundraising, and corporate appellate functions. All certified technical findings, indicators, and framework calibrations shall be managed under pre-established, objective scientific protocols designed to preserve methodology transparency and systemic reversibility. All such technical processes shall be conducted subject to applicable state and federal law, due process, rigorous data-quality safeguards, and the Board’s nondelegable fiduciary oversight.

Section 1.6 — Restrictions and Tax-Exempt Compliance

(a) Private Inurement and Benefit: No part of the net earnings of the Institute shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its directors, officers, founders, or other private persons, except that the Institute shall be authorized and empowered to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of the exempt purposes set forth in its Articles of Incorporation. All transactions involving potentially disqualified persons shall strictly comply with the Institute's Conflict of Interest Policy and applicable federal excess benefit transaction rules under Internal Revenue Code Section 4958.(b) Political Activities: The Institute shall not participate in, or intervene in (including the publishing or distribution of statements), any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office.(c) Legislative Activities: No substantial part of the activities of the Institute shall be the carrying on of propaganda, or otherwise attempting, to influence legislation, except as otherwise permitted under Internal Revenue Code Section 501(h). Any election under Section 501(h) or modification of legislative expenditure thresholds requires the explicit approval of the Board of Directors.

Section 1.7 — Powers, Offices, and Global Facilities

The Institute shall possess all corporate powers, rights, and privileges available to a public benefit corporation organized under Chapter 355 of the Revised Statutes of Missouri, consistent with its exempt purposes under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. The Institute may establish and maintain its principal corporate office, regional offices, global research programs, and decentralized Living Labs at any location within or outside the United States, as determined by the Board of Directors to best serve the international public interest.

Article II — No Statutory Members

Section 2.1 — No Voting Members

The Institute shall have no members with voting rights within the meaning of the Missouri Nonprofit Corporation Act. Fellows, affiliates, licensees, advisors, participating institutions, and representatives of Living Labs shall not acquire corporate voting rights by reason of that status.

Article III — Board of Directors

Section 3.1 — Authority and Role

All corporate powers shall be exercised by or under the authority of, and the affairs of the Institute managed under the direction of, the Board of Directors (the "Board"). The Board retains ultimate fiduciary oversight and all authority that applicable law makes nondelegable.

Section 3.2 — Boundary Between Oversight and Technical Determinations

The Board shall approve the Institute's mission, strategy, annual budget, risk appetite, institutional policies, senior executive appointments, and systems of accountability. Pursuant to the Board’s authority to create committees and delegate operational oversight under Section 355.401, RSMo, and except when necessary to discharge its nondelegable legal and fiduciary duties, the Board shall not substitute its own judgment for an individual scientific calibration, data certification, band classification, or trigger determination rendered by the c-ECO System. Technical and scientific challenges shall proceed through the independent review process adopted under Article VII of these Bylaws. The Board may require corrective process, additional verification, or the temporary suspension of an unsafe or unlawful protocol, provided that the Board shall document the legal and evidentiary basis for doing so in its official corporate minutes.

Section 3.3 — Number, Independence, and Qualifications

The Board shall consist of no fewer than three (3) and no more than fifteen (15) directors, with the exact number to be fixed from time to time by resolution of the Board. Directors need not be residents of the state of incorporation. A majority of directors then in office shall at all times be independent, meaning they are not compensated employees of the Institute, not under the supervision or control of a compensated employee, and have no material financial relationship with the Institute other than reimbursement of reasonable expenses incurred in service to the Institute.

Section 3.4 — Election and Terms

Directors shall be elected by the affirmative vote of a majority of the directors then in office. Each director shall serve a term of three (3) years, or until a successor is duly elected and qualified, or until earlier resignation, removal, or death. Terms shall be staggered as reasonably practicable so that approximately one-third of directorships are subject to election each year. Directors may be elected to no more than three (3) consecutive full terms, after which a director shall be ineligible for re-election until at least one (1) year has elapsed, unless waived by a two-thirds vote of the directors then in office.

Section 3.5 — Resignation, Removal, and Vacancies

(a) Resignation: A director may resign at any time by delivering written notice to the Chair or Secretary of the Board. A resignation is effective when the notice is delivered unless the notice specifies a later effective date.(b) Removal: In accordance with Section 355.346, RSMo, any director may be removed, with or without cause, by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the directors then in office at a meeting called for that purpose, provided that notice of the meeting states that the purpose, or one of the purposes, of the meeting is the removal of the director. A simple majority of the directors then in office may remove any director who has missed two (2) consecutive regular Board meetings or more than half of all Board meetings within a single calendar year.(c) Vacancies: Any vacancy occurring on the Board, including a vacancy resulting from an increase in the number of directors, may be filled from a slate of candidates recommended by the Governance Committee. Pursuant to Section 355.361, RSMo, the vacancy shall be filled by the affirmative vote of a majority of the remaining directors, even if the remaining directors constitute less than a quorum of the Board. If the directors remaining in office constitute fewer than a quorum, they may fill the vacancy by the affirmative vote of a majority of all the directors remaining in office. Each director so elected shall serve for the unexpired term of their predecessor.

Section 3.6 — Meetings

The Board shall meet at least quarterly, including an annual meeting, at such times and places (including by remote communication) as the Board may determine. Special meetings may be called by the Board Chair, any two voting directors, or the President upon five days' notice, or two days' notice for a stated urgent matter, which shall in all cases constitute at least two full calendar days, consistent with Section 355.386, RSMo. Participation may occur by communications through which all participants can hear one another.

Section 3.7 — Notice and Waiver

Notice of any meeting may be given by mail, e-mail, or other reasonable means. A director’s attendance at a meeting constitutes waiver of notice of that meeting, except where the director attends for the express purpose of objecting to the transaction of business on the ground that the meeting was not lawfully called. The notice under Section 3.6 (above) shall include an agenda and any document requiring Board vote(s).

Section 3.8 — Quorum and Voting

A majority of the number of voting directors specified or fixed in accordance with these Bylaws shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business. Unless a greater vote is required by law, the Articles of Incorporation, or these Bylaws, the affirmative vote of a majority of the voting directors present at a meeting at which a quorum is present shall be the act of the Board. Pursuant to Section 355.406, RSMo, any action required or permitted to be taken at a Board meeting may be taken without a meeting if the action is taken by all members of the Board through a unanimous written consent detailing the action taken, signed by each director, and filed with the corporate minutes.

Section 3.9 — Chief Executive Officer as Ex Officio Participant

The Institute's chief executive officer (whether the President or the Executive Director, as determined by the Board under Section 4.3) shall attend Board meetings as a non-voting participant ex officio, shall receive the same notices and materials as voting directors, and may participate in deliberations, except during executive sessions concerning the chief executive officer's compensation, performance, removal, litigation involving the chief executive officer, or a conflict of interest. This participation does not confer membership in the Board, voting rights, or director status. The chief executive officer shall not be counted for quorum purposes and shall not receive director compensation.

Section 3.10 — Compensation

Directors shall serve without compensation for their service as directors, except that the Board may authorize reimbursement of reasonable expenses incurred in connection with Board duties. Nothing in this Section shall preclude a director from being reasonably compensated for services rendered to the Institute in a capacity other than as a director; compensation for separate professional services must be approved in advance by disinterested directors under Article VIII and applicable tax law.

Section 3.11 — Participation by Remote Communication

Directors may participate in any meeting by conference telephone, video conference, or similar means by which all persons participating can simultaneously hear one another, and such participation constitutes presence in person at the meeting.

Section 3.12 — Action Without a Meeting

Any action required or permitted to be taken at a Board meeting may be taken without a meeting if all directors consent in writing (including by e-mail), and the writing or writings are filed with the minutes of the Board.

Article IV — Officers and Executive Management

Section 4.1 — Required and Additional Officers

The officers of the Institute shall be those established under Section 355.431, RSMo, and shall include a Board Chair (who shall also serve as the Presiding Officer of the Board), a President, a Secretary, and a Treasurer. The Board may also appoint a Vice President, an Executive Director, and such other officers as it deems necessary.

For purposes of Section 355.456, RSMo, officers are classified as follows:

  • Category 1 officers: the Presiding Officer of the Board (Board Chair) and the President;
  • Category 2 officers: the Vice President, the Secretary, the Treasurer, and the Executive Director.

The Board Chair, Secretary, and Treasurer shall be voting directors. The same person may hold more than one office to the extent permitted by law, except that: (a) the Board Chair shall not simultaneously serve as President or Executive Director; and (b) the President and the Executive Director may be the same person or different persons, as determined by the Board.

Section 4.2 — Board Chair & Presiding Officer of the Board

The Board Chair is the principal governance officer of the Board, the presiding officer of the Board for all purposes under Section 355.456, RSMo, presides at Board meetings, supports effective fiduciary oversight, and has no unilateral authority to direct staff, Living Labs, scientific classifications, licenses, or programs unless expressly authorized by the Board. The Board Chair shall not receive compensation for service as Chair or as a director, but may receive reimbursement of expenses under Section 3.9.

Section 4.3 — President and Executive Director

(a) The President shall preside over the Board but shall not serve as chief executive officer or exercise day-to-day operational authority.

(b) The Board may appoint the Founder or another qualified person as Executive Director. The Executive Director shall serve as chief executive officer, may receive reasonable compensation, and shall have responsibility for the day-to-day leadership, administration, personnel, programs, contracts, partnerships, and implementation of the Board-approved strategy and budget. The Executive Director shall report to the Board as a body and not to the President or any individual director.

(c) The Executive Director shall participate in Board meetings ex officio and without vote and shall not be counted for quorum purposes. The Executive Director may be removed only by the Board, subject to any applicable employment agreement. The Founder shall not simultaneously serve as President while serving as Executive Director.

Section 4.4 — Secretary and Treasurer

The Secretary shall prepare minutes of all Board and shall ensure that accurate minutes of committee meetings are kept, ensure that required notices are given, maintain the Institute’s corporate records, authenticate records of the corporation, and certify official actions of the Board as needed. The Treasurer shall oversee the Institute’s finances, ensure that accurate financial books and records are maintained, present financial reports to the Board no less frequently than quarterly, and oversee preparation of the annual budget and required tax filings, in coordination with staff and any independent accountant retained by the Institute.

Section 4.5 — Election and Removal

Officers are elected annually and serve until a successor is qualified, resignation, death, or removal. Consistent with Section 355.446, RSMo, the Board may remove an officer at any time with or without cause. Removal from office does not by itself terminate employment, contractual remedies, Founder status, ownership of Founder Background IP, or rights under a separate agreement. Employment protections, including any definition of Cause, cure period, severance, and transition rights, must be stated in a written contract.

Section 4.6 — Executive Leadership Council

The chief executive officer (whether the President or the Executive Director, as determined by the Board under Section 4.3) shall chair an Executive Leadership Council as a management body, not a committee of the Board. Its standing portfolios shall include: (a) President/Executive Director and Framework Stewardship; (b) Living Labs and TDR–TFP Implementation; and (c) Fellowship and Academic Affairs. The Board shall approve its charter. The Council may coordinate delegated management matters but may not exercise powers reserved to the Board or independently certify or adjudicate its own work.

Section 4.7 — Continuity and Acting Authority

The Board shall approve an executive succession and continuity policy. The President may designate acting portfolio leadership for temporary absences, subject to Board confirmation for any appointment exceeding ninety days. No acting appointment transfers Founder status or Founder Background IP.

Article V — Founder and Framework Stewardship

Section 5.1 — Recognition

The Institute recognizes Jacqueline Giselle Goncalves Alves (also known professionally as Jacqueline A. Ennis) as its founder (the "Founder") and originating legal architect of the pre-existing juridical components of the c-ECO framework. Founder status is historical and personal and is not a directorship, corporate membership, employment guarantee, or ownership interest in charitable assets.

Section 5.2 — Framework Steward

While willing and able to serve, the Founder shall be designated Founding Framework Steward. The Steward safeguards conceptual coherence, provenance, canonical terminology, version history, and fidelity of the Institute's implementation to the public-interest purposes of the framework. Operational authority associated with the role is subject to Board-approved budgets, law, conflicts procedures, and the written Framework Stewardship Charter.

Section 5.2A — Permissible Roles of the Founder

The Founder may simultaneously serve as Board Chair and as Founding Framework Steward. The Founder may also enter into arms-length consulting agreements and intellectual-property license agreements with the Institute, subject to approval by disinterested directors under Article VIII. The Founder shall not simultaneously serve as Executive Director.

Section 5.3 — Founder Background IP

"Founder Background IP" means copyrightable expression, legal drafting, documentation, know-how, trademarks or other protectable material demonstrably created or acquired by the Founder outside the scope of Institute employment or before its contribution to the Institute, as specifically identified in a signed schedule. No provision of these Bylaws constitutes an assignment. Ownership, licenses, improvements, attribution, termination, and permitted uses shall be governed only by a separate signed Intellectual Property and Framework License Agreement.

Section 5.4 — Institute-Developed IP

Subject to written agreements and applicable law, intellectual property created by employees within the scope of employment or commissioned and paid for by the Institute shall be held for the Institute's charitable purposes. Joint, derivative, volunteer, fellow, and partner works must be addressed by signed agreements before material development begins.

Section 5.5 — Removal and Separation

Removal of the Founder as President, Executive Director, officer, employee, or program leader shall not erase Founder attribution, transfer Founder Background IP, or terminate licenses except as expressly provided in the applicable signed agreement. Nothing in this Article prevents the Board from exercising the removal authority required or permitted by law.

Section 5.6 — International Framework Representation

While willing and able to serve, the Founding Framework Steward shall lead the Institute's technical, conceptual, and framework-integrity representation in any Institute-sponsored preparatory dialogue, expert process, diplomatic initiative, or negotiation concerning a future intergovernmental C-ECO organization. The Board retains authority over legal commitments, expenditures, employment, fiduciary decisions, and positions formally binding the Institute. No other representative may waive, transfer, materially redefine, or represent a modification of the Founder Background IP or canonical framework as having the Founder's approval without the Founder's express written consent.

Article VI — Technical and Programmatic Governance

Section 6.1 — Institutional Bodies

The Institute shall maintain the functional separation described in this Article. These bodies are institutional or management bodies and are not committees of the Board unless separately constituted with only directors as required by law. They shall not exercise powers legally reserved to the Board.

Section 6.2 — Scientific and Calibration Council

An independent Scientific and Calibration Council shall oversee scientific standards, calibration principles, methodological validation, and versioned technical guidance. Its charter shall establish multidisciplinary competence, staggered terms, conflicts rules, transparent records, and protections against influence by operators, licensees, donors, or vendors.

Section 6.3 — Technical Curatorship Chamber

A Technical Curatorship Chamber ("TCC") shall provide independent technical certification and review under applicable contracts and protocols. It shall include scientific, sectoral, and legal competence; have access to required monitoring records; disclose conflicts; and receive compensation, if any, through arrangements designed to protect independence. Persons employed by or materially dependent upon a contracting operator shall not adjudicate that operator's certification.

Section 6.4 — Living Labs Directorate

The Living Labs Directorate shall implement, test, and document the framework in real-world settings under the Executive Leadership Council. It shall not exclusively validate, certify, audit, or adjudicate its own work. Location and operational design of individual Labs shall be established by policy or agreement, not by these Bylaws.

Section 6.5 — Fellowship and Academic Affairs

The Fellowship and Academic Affairs function shall manage global education, supervised applied projects, research calls, fellow selection, and academic partnerships under published standards and a Board-approved charter.

Section 6.6 — Data Governance Office

The Institute shall maintain a Data Governance function responsible for data classification, provenance, custodianship, security, privacy, sovereignty, retention, access, interoperability, incident response, and vendor review. No software vendor or CRM platform shall determine the Institute's substantive data-governance rules. Government, sensor, community, Indigenous, personal, confidential, and controlled data shall be handled under applicable law and specific agreements.

Section 6.7 — Licensing, Certification, and Seals

The Institute may administer licenses, certifications, seals, and authorized implementations in furtherance of exempt purposes. Commercial development and applicant support shall be separated from final certification decisions. Published criteria, conflicts controls, monitoring, suspension, appeal, and misuse procedures shall be maintained. Net revenues shall support the Institute's exempt purposes.

Section 6.8 — Public Observatory

The Institute shall maintain a public-interest transparency function for appropriate methodologies, reports, risk classifications, audit trails, and civic access. Publication is subject to lawful protections for privacy, security, Indigenous data governance, contractual confidentiality, and protected intellectual property. Environmental and social monitoring information shall be presumptively accessible when law and legitimate safety interests permit.

Section 6.9 — Independence of the c-ECO System

The c-ECO System is functionally distinct from the corporate governance of the Institute. Corporate officers and the Board administer legal, financial, employment, strategic, and fiduciary matters; technical bodies administer calibration, certification, version integrity, and individual technical determinations within their delegated mandates. No donor, operator, licensee, software vendor, officer, director acting individually, or commercial function may direct the result of an individual technical determination. Nothing in this Section divests the Board of authority or duties that applicable law makes nondelegable.

Section 6.10 — Institutional Charters

The Board shall cause written charters to be maintained for the Executive Leadership Council, Scientific and Calibration Council, TCC, Independent Review Panel, Living Labs, Fellowship and Academic Affairs, Data Governance Office, Licensing and Seal function, and Observatory. Charters shall implement, and may not contradict or materially weaken, the separation and independence required by these Bylaws.

Article VII — Independent Review, Triggers, and Accountability

Section 7.1 — Separation of Functions

To ensure operational integrity and eliminate structural conflicts of interest, no single individual, committee, or corporate body within or contracted by the Institute shall simultaneously serve as the sole operator, certifier, auditor, and appellate decision-maker for the same project, dataset, or classification framework. Operational charters adopted by the Board of Directors shall explicitly define mandatory recusal protocols, substitute reviewers, documentation requirements, and formal escalation pathways.

Section 7.2 — Trigger Integrity

Material technical trigger criteria and corresponding response categories shall be established in advance through version-controlled scientific protocols and formal contractual terms. Protective governance interventions designated as immediate, temporary, and operationally reversible shall not be waived, bypassed, or delayed solely for commercial convenience or financial expediency. Any deviation or exception must be lawful under applicable state and federal law, fully documented in the corporate records, independently reviewed by the c-ECO System, time-limited, and accompanied by alternative protective measures of equivalent or greater efficacy.

Section 7.3 — Review Without Governance Delay

The Board of Directors shall establish and maintain an expedited, independent technical review process for disputed data, classifications, or trigger events. Unless otherwise mandated by an applicable governing contract or governing law, the initiation of a formal challenge shall not automatically suspend or stay temporary protective measures deemed necessary by the c-ECO System to preserve systemic reversibility and ecological integrity. Irreversible final sanctions, technical de-certifications, or permanent dispositions shall be subject to appropriate administrative due process as set forth by institutional policy.

Section 7.4 — Audit Trails and Verification

The Institute shall enforce protocols requiring the maintenance of immutable or tamper-evident technical records, methodology versioning controls, mandatory data retention periods, and independent third-party verification. Technical corrections or updates to data frameworks shall be executed exclusively via supplemental or ledgered entry rather than undisclosed overwriting. Technical retention periods and data access tiers shall be systematically governed by approved institutional policy and contract.

Section 7.5 — Whistleblower and Community Access

In alignment with the Institute’s tax-exempt public purposes, the Institute shall maintain confidential, non-retaliatory reporting channels. The Board shall implement proportionate procedures enabling affected communities, qualified civil-society participants, and independent researchers to submit technical observations and request formal reviews, subject to defined rules governing legal standing, confidentiality, safety, and anti-abuse metrics.

Section 7.6 — Public Irreversible Risk Taxonomy

The C-ECO Irreversible Risk Taxonomy (the "Taxonomy") shall be maintained as a publicly accessible, authoritative common language for the identification, classification, and governance of irreversible and complex systemic risks. The public layer of the Taxonomy shall include its nineteen (19) foundational domains, fundamental concepts and definitions, risk classifications, relevant environmental, economic, and financial relationships, core reversibility principles, intervention categories, the general L0–L3/GIH structure, official versions, and an accurate public history of amendments and revisions.

Section 7.7 — Legal Effect of Public Access

Public access to the Taxonomy is granted solely to permit reading, academic citation, scientific research, teaching, and public-policy development under published public-use terms. Public access shall not, by itself, confer any license or authorization to formally implement the TDR–TFP methodology, utilize the SIL 19×19 platform or successor software systems, operate a recognized Living Lab, train or certify official Fellows, issue a C-ECO technical determination, represent any external system as officially integrated or approved, sub-license corporate assets, or utilize any C-ECO certification mark, commercial license, or corporate seal.

Section 7.8 — Controlled Normative Standard

The official standard of the Taxonomy and its related frameworks shall be maintained strictly through version control, formalized amendment criteria, peer-reviewed scientific evaluation, proportionate public consultation, official translation controls, and historical provenance records. The Institute shall actively protect against unauthorized or modified materials being represented as official standards. While the public may read and cite official versions, only the competent c-ECO authority designated by the Board may officially declare a text, translation, classification, or implementation standard as legally or technically binding upon the ecosphere.

Section 7.9 — Licensed Implementation

During the operational phase of the Institute as a Missouri nonprofit corporation, the Institute shall administer, directly or through authorized corporate arrangements, practitioner licenses, institutional implementation licenses, software licenses, Living Lab recognition and accreditations, Fellowship authorizations, trainer and assessor credentials, certification-mark and seal licenses, and implementation contracts. All such licensing frameworks shall be structured exclusively to further the Institute’s tax-exempt public purposes, utilize objective and published criteria where practicable, preserve absolute technical independence, and structurally separate an applicant's development or sales activities from final technical certification and appellate decisions.

Section 7.10 — Future Intergovernmental Phase

The Institute may conduct research, convene stakeholders, and support preparatory frameworks looking toward the potential establishment of a future intergovernmental C-ECO organization established by international treaty. Upon the lawful establishment of such an organization, state-level normative authority, sovereign participation, and treaty implementation may be transitioned to that international body.Technical, educational, software, research, capacity-building, and commercial licensing operations may remain with the Institute under a joint governance and licensing agreement. Any such agreement must explicitly protect public access, independent scientific calibration, operational continuity, Founder Background Intellectual Property, Institute-developed assets, and the permanent tax-exempt charitable purposes of the Institute. Sovereign state participation in the international framework shall arise strictly through treaty or public-law instruments and shall not be treated as a commercial license.

Section 7.11 — No Automatic Transfer to Future Organization

No provision within these Bylaws regarding a future intergovernmental organization shall constitute a present assignment, dedication, transfer, or exclusive license of the Taxonomy, the governance framework, proprietary software, corporate marks, Institute assets, or Founder Background Intellectual Property. Any future allocation of authority, rights, or assets requires an express written instrument approved by the Board of Directors as a Protected Matter and must strictly comply with Missouri charitable-asset laws, Internal Revenue Code Section 501(c)(3) dissolution and diversion rules, and applicable intellectual property laws.

Section 7.12 — International Transition Conditions

The Institute shall not formally sponsor, endorse, execute, or legally recommend an establishment agreement, treaty text, headquarters arrangement, succession instrument, or asset-transfer agreement for a future intergovernmental C-ECO organization unless the proposed international arrangement, to the extent reasonably achievable:(a) Preserves permanent, meaningful public access to the Taxonomy;(b) Protects independent scientific calibration, certification, compliance metrics, and review from geopolitical or commercial interference;(c) Recognizes the Institute as a continuing technical, academic, research, capacity-building, and implementation center, or establishes a functionally equivalent legally protected relationship;(d) Clearly distinguishes interstate sovereign normative authority from technical, educational, software, and commercial licensing;(e) Provides for a formal Joint Governance and Licensing Protocol where corporate and sovereign responsibilities overlap;(f) Fully preserves Founder Background Intellectual Property and existing lawful Institute rights except to the extent expressly licensed or transferred for fair market value or approved public benefit;(g) Establishes transparent amendment, translation, public consultation, and version-control procedures; and(h) Includes appropriate operational continuity, international dispute-resolution, withdrawal, transition, and charitable asset-protection arrangements.

Section 7.13 — Founder Participation Sought in Future Instruments

In any Institute-sponsored preparatory process looking toward an international framework, the Institute shall use reasonable best efforts to seek formal recognition of the original Founder as the originating legal architect and Founding Framework Steward. The Institute shall further seek to establish an appropriate, continuing non-compensatory technical, consultative, observer, or curatorial role for the Founder within the future international architecture to preserve institutional continuity. This Section binds the Institute's internal administrative conduct and negotiating mandate only; it does not purport to bind any sovereign State, international organization, treaty conference, or third-party entity, and it does not guarantee appointment to any organ created by a future international treaty.

Section 7.14 — International Transition Working Group

The President may establish an International Transition Working Group, chaired by the Founding Framework Steward while willing and able to serve, to develop institutional options, draft technical materials, conduct stakeholder consultations, and advise the Executive Leadership Council and the Board of Directors. The Working Group is an administrative advisory body, is not a committee of the Board of Directors under Section 355.401, RSMo, and possesses no corporate power to legally bind the Institute, its assets, or any participating government. Its composition shall include appropriate international-law, scientific, economic, governmental, Indigenous or community, and institutional expertise as the relevant phase requires, with members appointed by the President in consultation with the Board.

Article VIII — Board Committees, Conflicts, and Compliance

Section 8.1 — Board Committees

The Board may establish committees composed of two or more voting directors. Standing committees shall include Finance and Audit and Governance and Nominating, unless the Board documents why consolidation is appropriate during an initial organizational period. Committee authority remains subject to statutory limitations.

Section 8.2 — Conflicts of Interest

The Institute shall maintain and consistently enforce a written conflicts policy covering directors, officers, key employees, technical reviewers, certifiers, fellows with decision authority, and other covered persons. Interested persons shall disclose the interest, recuse as required, and shall not improperly influence deliberations. Disinterested decision-makers shall determine fairness and institutional benefit, with contemporaneous minutes.

A director who is also an employee or contractor of the Institute shall not vote on, participate in deliberations concerning, or be counted for quorum purposes regarding: (a) the director's own compensation, employment terms, or benefits; (b) any transaction in which the director has a material financial interest; or (c) any amendment to provisions of these Bylaws or the Articles of Incorporation affecting the director's rights as Founder or Framework Steward. Such matters shall be decided solely by the disinterested directors.

For the avoidance of doubt, any consulting agreement, professional services agreement, or intellectual-property license agreement to which a director is a party shall be treated as a transaction in which the director has a material financial interest for purposes of this Section.

Section 8.3 — Compensation and Related-Party Transactions

Compensation and transactions involving the Founder, officers, directors, key employees, or related persons shall be approved in advance by disinterested directors using appropriate comparability information and contemporaneous documentation. Intellectual-property licenses from an insider must be demonstrably fair, reasonable, and in furtherance of exempt purposes.

Section 8.4 — Required Policies

The Board shall adopt and periodically review policies addressing conflicts, whistleblowers, document retention, data governance and security, compensation, research integrity, licensing and certification, financial controls, gifts, and joint ventures.

Article IX — Financial Management, Records, and Indemnification

Section 9.1 — Budget and Controls

The Board shall adopt an annual budget and designate signing and contracting authority. Institute funds shall be deposited in approved depositories and used only in furtherance of exempt purposes.

Section 9.2 — Records and Reporting

The Institute shall keep correct and complete books and records of account and minutes of the proceedings of the Board and its committees, and shall make such records available for inspection as required by applicable law. The Institute shall keep of a copy of the following records at its principal office: (1) articles or restated articles of incorporation and all amendments to them currently in effect; (2) bylaws or restated bylaws and all amendments to them currently in effect; (3) resolutions adopted by its board of directors; (4) minutes of all Board meetings; (5) all financial statements furnished for the past three years under section 355.846; (6) list of the names and business or home addresses of its current directors and officers; (7) Its most recent corporate registration report delivered to the secretary of state under section 355.856; and (8) appropriate financial statements of all income and expenses. The Treasurer, in coordination with staff, shall provide the Board with financial statements no less frequently than quarterly and shall arrange for an annual review or audit of the Institute’s financial statements by an independent accountant when required by contract, grant, law or when directed by the Board. An independent audit or review shall be obtained when required by law, funder condition, or Board determination.

Section 9.3 — Indemnification and Insurance

To the fullest extent permitted by law, the Institute shall indemnify directors, officers, employees, and agents who acted in good faith and in a manner reasonably believed to be in the Institute's best interests. The Institute may maintain directors and officers, professional liability, cyber, and other appropriate insurance.

Article X — Protected Matters and Amendments

Section 10.1 — Ordinary Amendments

Except for Protected Matters, these Bylaws may be amended by two-thirds of voting directors then in office after at least ten days' written notice stating the proposed changes.

Section 10.2 — Protected Matters

The following are Protected Matters: (a) material alteration of the purposes or hybrid architecture in Sections 1.2–1.5; (b) amendment or repeal of Articles V, VI, VII, or this Article X; (c) assignment, exclusive license, encumbrance, abandonment, or disposition of core framework assets outside the ordinary course; (d) elimination or material weakening of independent scientific calibration, certification, compliance, or review; (e) merger, conversion, dissolution, or transfer of substantially all framework activities; (f) action purporting to transfer Founder Background IP without a signed assignment; (g) withdrawal of the Taxonomy from meaningful public access; (h) authorization of unrestricted official or commercial implementation without the controls required by Section 7.7; (i) allocation of normative authority or core assets to a future intergovernmental organization; or (j) formal Institute endorsement or execution of a treaty, establishment agreement, succession arrangement, or Joint Governance and Licensing Protocol that materially departs from Sections 7.10–7.13.

Section 10.3 — Approval Standard

A Protected Matter requires approval of at least three-fourths of voting directors then in office, including a majority of disinterested directors, the Founder's prior written consent while the Founder is living and capable of giving consent, and any additional approval required by the Articles of Incorporation. The Institute should amend its Articles, as permitted by Section 355.606, RSMo, to protect this narrowly defined approval right. Until such an Articles amendment becomes effective, this Section does not represent that a bylaw provision alone creates an irrevocable external approval right against a later lawful amendment.

Section 10.4 — Fiduciary and Tax Savings Clause

No consent, stewardship, licensing, or protected-matter provision shall be construed to require a director to violate fiduciary duties, permit private inurement or excess benefit, or prevent action required by law. When emergency action is legally required, the Board shall choose the least intrusive lawful measure and preserve contractual, attribution, and IP rights to the maximum extent permitted.

Article XI — Dissolution and General Provisions

Section 11.1 — Dissolution

After liabilities are paid or provided for, the Institute's remaining assets shall be distributed exclusively for one or more exempt purposes under Section 501(c)(3), or to government for a public purpose. Property held under license or subject to valid reversion, restriction, or custodial terms shall be handled according to those terms and applicable law; no charitable asset shall be distributed for private benefit.

Section 11.2 — Construction and Severability

These Bylaws shall be construed consistently with Chapter 355, RSMo, and Section 501(c)(3). If a provision is invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions remain effective. Headings do not affect interpretation.

Section 11.3 — Hierarchy

In the event of conflict, applicable law and the Articles of Incorporation control over these Bylaws; these Bylaws control over Board resolutions, charters, policies, manuals, and protocols; and signed contracts govern the parties' contractual rights subject to that hierarchy.

Section 11.4 — Effective Date

These Initial Bylaws become effective upon adoption by the Sole Incorporator pursuant to Sections 355.111 and 355.116, RSMo. Subsequent acknowledgment or ratification by the initial Board does not condition their effectiveness.
